# Default writing style (adapt to the organisation's locale)

| Topic | en-GB default | en-US default |
|---|---|---|
| Spelling | colour, organisation, centre, licence (noun) | color, organization, center, license |
| Dates | 2 October 2026; avoid 02/10/26 | October 2, 2026; avoid 10/2/26 |
| Times | 9am, 2:30pm, 24-hour for schedules if needed | 9 a.m. or 9 AM (pick one), 2:30 p.m. |
| Quotes | Single 'quotes', double for quotes within quotes (or follow existing house style) | Double "quotes" |
| Serial comma | Optional; recommend it for clarity | Yes |
| Numbers | One to nine in words, 10 and above as numerals; always numerals for data, money, percentages | Same |
| Large numbers | 1,000; 2.5 million | Same |
| Percent | 45% | 45% (or "percent" in formal prose) |
| Currency | £10, £10.50, €5; avoid ".00" | $10, $10.50 |
| Phone | +44 20 7946 0000 | +1 (555) 010-0000 |
| Ranges | 10 to 20 in prose; 10–20 (en dash) in tables | Same |

## Capitalisation

- Sentence case for headings, buttons, menus, labels and email subjects.
- Capitalise proper nouns, product names and job titles only before a name.
- Avoid ALL CAPS in body text: it is harder to read and some screen readers spell it out. Use CSS text-transform for small labels if needed.

## Plain language

- Use short sentences (average 15–20 words; none over 30). One idea per sentence.
- Use the active voice. Put the most important information first.
- Use common words: "use", not "utilise"; "help", not "facilitate"; "about", not "approximately".
- Define acronyms on first use: "web content accessibility guidelines (WCAG)".
- Write for a reading age of 9–11 (UK) or US grade 6–8 for public content.

## Links and calls to action

- Link text says where it goes: "Read our accessibility statement", not "click here".
- Use verbs for buttons: "Download the logo pack", "Book a demo".
- Say if a link opens a file, with its type and size: "Brand guidelines (PDF, 2 MB)".

## Inclusive language

- Ask people and communities how they want to be described. Default to people-first ("disabled people" is preferred in the UK social model; "people with disabilities" is common in the US). Follow the community you are writing for.
- Avoid: "suffers from", "wheelchair-bound", "the disabled", "normal" (as opposed to disabled), and "crazy", "lame", "blind to", "tone-deaf" as metaphors.
- Use gender-neutral terms: "they", "team", "everyone", "chair", "staff", "people".
- Avoid ageist, culturally specific idioms and sports or military metaphors where they may exclude people.
- Do not describe people by a characteristic that is not relevant.

## Accessible formatting

- Use descriptive headings in a logical order, and lists for steps.
- Do not rely on sensory characteristics: say "Select Save", not "click the green button on the right".
- Emoji and hashtags: see the accessibility baseline (CamelCase, emoji at the end, sparingly).
