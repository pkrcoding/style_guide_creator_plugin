# Co-branding and partnerships

Much of our work is done with city councils, utilities, universities and other climate-tech organisations. When our logo appears next to theirs, the layout tells people who is responsible for what. Getting it right builds trust with public bodies; getting it wrong can breach their rules on endorsement and procurement.

The single most important rule: **each partner's own brand guidelines govern their logo.** Ask for their guidelines and logo files at the start of every partnership, and follow them exactly.

## Relationship types

| Relationship | Example | Logo hierarchy | Wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partnership of equals | A joint research project with a university | Equal optical size, side by side | "In partnership with" |
| Client-led (we are the supplier) | A heat-risk dashboard for a city council | Client logo leads; ours smaller, or credit in text only | "Powered by Northwind Labs" or "Data by Northwind Labs", only if the client agrees |
| We lead, partner supports | Our report with a partner's data | Our logo leads; partner logo smaller | "With support from" or "Data from" |
| Sponsorship | We sponsor an event | Event brand leads; our logo in the sponsor area at the agreed size | "Sponsored by" |
| Endorsed sub-brand | A Northwind Labs product with its own name | Product name leads; "by Northwind Labs" below or beside it | "by Northwind Labs" |

## Lock-up rules

1. **Align** logos on a shared horizontal centre line, or a shared baseline if both are wordmarks.
2. **Separate** logos with a thin vertical divider line in neutral-300 (or white at reduced opacity on dark backgrounds) or with a short phrase such as "in partnership with". Never let the logos touch.
3. **Space:** the gap between each logo and the divider is at least our clear space (chapter 3). Our clear space then also surrounds the whole lock-up.
4. **Equal partners** have equal *optical* size, not equal width. Our logo is wide (about five times as wide as it is tall), so match the visual weight by eye; compact or square partner logos usually need to be taller than ours.
5. **Order:** the lead organisation goes first (left, or top in a stacked lock-up). For equal partners, put the host or contracting party first, or use alphabetical order.
6. **Colour:** both logos in full colour, or both in single colour. Do not mix one full-colour logo with one single-colour logo.
7. **Background:** a white or light neutral background usually suits both full-colour logos. On Polar Night or Fjord Teal, use our single-colour light logo and the partner's approved reversed version.
8. **Never** merge our symbol with a partner's mark, recolour a partner logo in our colours, or make a new combined logo without approval from both organisations.

> **Do** ask for each partner's logo files and guidelines in vector format, and check you have the latest version.

> **Don't** download a partner's logo from a search engine or their website header.

## Working with government and public-sector logos

Councils, combined authorities, government departments and regulated utilities have strict rules on their identities.

- **Follow their guidelines exactly.** Many councils have their own brand guidelines; UK central government departments use their own identity rules and often do not allow their logo on supplier materials at all.
- **Endorsement:** a public body's logo next to ours can imply that they endorse our company or products. Most public bodies do not permit this outside an agreed joint project. Do not use a client logo in marketing, case studies, client-logo walls or tender responses without written permission from the client's communications team.
- **Coats of arms and crests** are often legally protected. Never use them unless the client supplies the artwork and approves the use.
- **Funded projects:** grants and innovation programmes (for example UK Research and Innovation or local growth funds) often require their logo and a funding statement in a set format. Follow the funder's publicity guidance.
- **Their documents, their template:** if a council publishes a report we contributed to, it uses their template. We appear as a credit line, for example "Analysis by Northwind Labs", unless agreed otherwise.

## Sponsorship

- Ask the organiser for the sponsor logo specification (size, colour version, file format) and supply files from the asset library (chapter 20).
- Supply our primary logo as vector (SVG for digital, PDF or EPS for print), plus the single-colour light version for dark backgrounds.
- Ask to see a proof of every item showing our logo before it is printed or published.

## Sub-brands and product names

*Proposed — confirm with leadership:* our brand architecture is **branded house**. Products are named descriptively and endorsed by the master brand, rather than given separate logos.

- Name products plainly: "Northwind [Product name]", for example "Northwind [Heat Risk]" (illustrative placeholder).
- Product names are set in Manrope next to or below the primary logo, separated by our clear space. Do not create a new symbol for a product.
- A product needs its own logo only if the Marketing team and leadership agree it will be sold or partnered separately.

## Partners using our logo

- Partners must use our logo files from the asset library and follow chapter 3 (clear space, minimum size, backgrounds and misuse).
- Partners describe the relationship accurately. They must not imply that we endorse their products unless agreed.

## Co-branding accessibility
- Both logos must reach at least 3:1 contrast against the background. If a partner's logo fails on the chosen background, change the background or use their reversed version; never change their colours.
- Alt text names both organisations and the relationship, for example "Northwind Labs in partnership with [Partner name]".
- The relationship is also stated in text (a credit line, caption or page content), not only shown through logos.
- Lock-ups on slides, web pages and social posts follow the same size, contrast and alt-text rules as our own logo.
