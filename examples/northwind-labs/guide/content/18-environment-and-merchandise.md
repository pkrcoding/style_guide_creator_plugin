# Signage, environment and merchandise

Physical items are seen at a distance, in poor light and in passing: a booth across a conference hall, a sign on a glass door, a lanyard at a council workshop. They need the simplest, boldest version of the brand, and they must work for people with low vision or who use a wheelchair.

The logo's strong geometry helps here. The circle-and-peak symbol stays recognisable when it is large, small, embroidered or cut from vinyl. Commission all physical items through the Marketing team (hello@northwindlabs.example) and get a proof signed off before production.

## Signage

### Sizing for viewing distance

As a rule of thumb, allow about 25 mm of cap height for every 10 m of viewing distance. Directional signs read at close range have a cap height of at least 15 mm.

| Viewing distance | Minimum cap height | Logo |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 2 m (door and room signs) | 15–20 mm | Symbol, or no logo |
| 5 m (corridor, reception) | 15–25 mm | Primary logo at least 150 mm wide |
| 10 m (building entrance) | 25 mm | Primary logo at least 400 mm wide |
| 20 m+ (exterior) | 50 mm and up | Primary logo or symbol, sized with the sign maker |

### Contrast and finish

- Aim for a light reflectance value (LRV) difference of at least 70 points between the text and the sign background, and a clear difference between the sign and the wall it sits on.
- Approved sign colourways: Polar Night text on white or neutral-50, white text on Polar Night, white text on Fjord Teal. Daybreak Amber is for accents only, never for sign text on light backgrounds.
- Use a matt, non-reflective finish. Gloss and glass-faced signs glare under spotlights.
- Paint and vinyl colours (for example RAL or NCS references) must be matched to a physical sample of the brand colours; do not pick them from a screen.

### Typography on signs

- Inter (or Manrope bold for short names), sentence case, left-aligned or centred on narrow signs.
- No condensed, light or italic weights, and no all-capitals text.

### Mounting, tactile and braille signs

For UK premises and venues, **BS 8300** and **Approved Document M** of the Building Regulations give guidance on sign mounting heights, contrast and tactile signs; the venue or landlord's access consultant confirms what applies. Where tactile or braille signs are required, use raised sans-serif characters and Grade 2 braille, and mount them within the height range the guidance sets. For any office or event outside the UK, check local rules (for example the ADA Standards in the US).

> **Do** confirm mounting heights and tactile requirements with the building's access consultant before ordering.

> **Don't** put sign text over a photograph or a gradient.

### Wayfinding pictograms

Use standard public-information symbols (ISO 7001), such as toilets, accessible toilet, lift, stairs and exits. Do not redraw them in a brand style; people recognise the standard shapes. Fire safety signs follow BS ISO 7010 and are never rebranded.

## Office branding

- **Reception wall:** the primary logo in dimensional letters or printed vinyl, full colour on a white or light neutral wall, or single-colour light on a Polar Night wall. Centre it at about eye height (around 1500–1600 mm to the centre).
- **Glass doors and walls:** apply manifestations (visibility markings) so people do not walk into the glass. Use a frosted band or a repeated symbol pattern at two heights, roughly 850–1000 mm and 1400–1600 mm from the floor, as Approved Document K and BS 8300 recommend; confirm with the building owner.
- **Meeting rooms:** room names in Inter on a simple sign. Avoid covering walls in logos; one brand moment per room is enough.

## Events and conference booths

| Item | Size | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Roll-up banner | 850 × 2000 mm | Logo in the top 400 mm; key message at 1000–1700 mm, where people read it; nothing important in the bottom 300 mm, which hides behind tables |
| Booth back wall | Per venue spec (often 3 × 2.5 m) | Logo top left or centred above head height; one headline; a QR code with the written short web address below it |
| Step-and-repeat backdrop | 2.4 × 2.4 m | Repeating symbol and primary logo, with partner logos per chapter 19 |
| Lanyard | 15–20 mm wide | Single-colour light logo repeated on Polar Night or Fjord Teal; breakaway safety clasp |
| Name badge | A6 or 100 × 75 mm | First name at least 24pt bold, organisation and role below; logo small at top; same layout for staff and guests |

- Use the single-colour light logo on Fjord Teal or Polar Night panels, and the full-colour logo only on white panels.
- Large Daybreak Amber panels are allowed for impact on booths; use Polar Night text and the single-colour dark logo on them.
- Print booth graphics on matt fabric or matt vinyl to avoid glare under hall lighting.
- Provide a seating area, step-free access to the booth and printed materials in large print on request.

## Vehicles

If we brand vehicles (for example field-survey vans), use the primary logo on white or light-grey bodywork, or the single-colour light logo on dark bodywork. Keep the logo on flat panels away from door seams and handles, and repeat the web address in Inter. Follow DVSA and local regulations for any required markings.

## Uniforms and embroidery

- Use the symbol or the primary logo, never smaller than 50 mm wide for the primary logo on clothing. Below that, the thin "LABS" letters fill in when stitched.
- Embroidery: use the single-colour version or the full-colour version with thread colours matched by the supplier to physical brand colour samples. Ask for a stitched sample before ordering.
- Garment colours: white, Polar Night-like navy or Fjord Teal-like teal from the supplier's range, chosen against a physical sample.

## Merchandise and promotional items

We are a climate business, so every item must be useful, durable and responsibly sourced.

- **Choose:** reusable bottles and cups, recycled or organic cotton bags, notebooks on recycled paper, FSC-certified items.
- **Avoid:** single-use plastics, novelty items people throw away, and anything that contradicts our climate work.
- Print the logo in one colour where possible: single-colour light on dark items, single-colour dark on light items. It reduces cost and print errors.
- Keep the logo above the print minimum in chapter 3 and give it full clear space. On very small items (pens, USB sticks), use the symbol.
- Ask suppliers for a pre-production sample and check colour, placement and quality before approving the run.

> **Do** choose fewer, better items that people will keep.

> **Don't** recolour the logo to match a product's colour; choose a product colour that suits an approved logo version.

## Packaging

If we ship physical products (for example sensor kits), packaging follows the print rules in chapter 16 and must carry any legally required text (such as safety, recycling and WEEE markings) at a legible size, at least 8pt, or the size the relevant regulation sets where that is the legal requirement and space genuinely does not allow 8pt.
