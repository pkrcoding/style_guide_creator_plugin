# Introduction

Northwind Labs turns climate data into decisions for city governments and utilities. The people we serve — sustainability officers, utility engineers, investors and partners — judge us on whether our work is clear, accurate and trustworthy. A consistent, accessible brand is part of that proof: every chart, report, slide and post should look and sound like it came from the same careful team.

## Why this guide exists

This guide sets out how to present Northwind Labs everywhere the brand appears: our logo, colours, typography, layout, imagery, data visualisation, motion, voice, writing style, digital products, social media, print, presentations, events and partnerships. It also explains the reasons behind each rule, so you can make good decisions in situations the guide does not cover.

It is built from one source of truth. The colour values, type sizes, spacing, logo files and social templates you see here are generated from the same data as the design tokens and the asset kit. If you copy a value from this guide, it will match the code and the files.

## Who it is for

- **Marketing and communications staff** writing campaigns, web pages, reports and social posts.
- **Designers and agencies** creating artwork, templates, motion and events.
- **Product designers and developers** building dashboards, data products and the website, using the design tokens.
- **Everyone else at Northwind Labs** who writes an email, builds a slide deck or speaks for us on LinkedIn.
- **Partners, suppliers and printers** who need to reproduce our identity correctly.

## How to use it

1. **Start with the foundations.** Read Brand foundations and Voice and tone first. They explain who we are and how we sound, and most other decisions follow from them.
2. **Find the chapter for your task.** Each chapter stands on its own: Logo, Colour, Typography and Social media are the ones most people need.
3. **Use the files, not screenshots.** Download logos, templates and tokens from the asset library listed in Legal, governance and assets. Never recreate the logo or retype colour values by eye.
4. **Check accessibility as you go.** The Accessibility chapter includes a checklist for every format.

## What is mandatory and what is flexible

| Mandatory (must follow) | Flexible (use judgement) |
|---|---|
| Logo artwork, clear space, minimum sizes and misuse rules | Layout composition within the grid |
| Approved colour pairings and contrast thresholds | Choice of supporting tints from the tonal scales |
| Typefaces, minimum text sizes and line spacing | Photography subjects within the imagery principles |
| Accessibility requirements in every chapter | Tone, adjusted to the context and audience |
| Legal lines, trademark and disclosure rules | Content pillars and campaign ideas on social |

Rules written as "must", "never" or in a **Don't** callout are mandatory. Rules written as "we recommend" or "usually" leave room for judgement, as long as the result stays accessible and recognisably Northwind Labs.

> **Do** ask the brand owner before you break a rule. There may be an approved exception, or the guide may need updating.

> **Don't** treat an earlier piece of work as precedent if it breaks this guide. Follow the guide, and tell us about the gap.

## Our accessibility commitment

We design everything to meet WCAG 2.2 AA as a minimum, because our audiences include people with low vision, colour-vision deficiency, cognitive and motor disabilities, and because many of our clients are public bodies with their own legal accessibility duties. Accessibility is built into every rule in this guide, not added as a separate step: colour pairings are tested, type sizes have minimums, charts never rely on colour alone, and every image and video needs alt text or captions. If something you are making cannot meet these standards, ask for help before you publish.

## Getting help

Questions, approvals and requests for new assets go to the Marketing team at hello@northwindlabs.example. If you find a mistake, a gap or a rule that no longer works, tell us. The guide is reviewed on a regular cycle, described in Legal, governance and assets.
