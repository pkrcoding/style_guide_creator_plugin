# Legal, governance and assets

Our name and logo are how families, volunteers and funders know that something really comes from us. Clear rules on ownership, approvals and files protect that trust, and stop out-of-date or unofficial versions spreading.

## Trademarks and our name

*Proposed — confirm with leadership and a legal adviser:* We have not confirmed whether the name or logo is a registered trade mark.

- Until registration is confirmed, **do not** use the ® symbol. Using ® for an unregistered mark is an offence in the UK.
- You may use ™ to show we claim the name and logo as our marks, but it is not needed on everyday materials. If used, place it once, on the first or most prominent use only.
- Always write our name in full as **Harbourside Community Trust** on first use. After that, *Harbourside Community Trust* or *the Trust* is fine. Do not shorten it to an acronym in public materials; acronyms are harder for readers with English as a second language.
- Never alter, abbreviate or add words to the logo.

## Required legal lines

UK charities must state their registered status on many documents. Our details have not yet been supplied, so use these placeholders until the Communications team confirms them:

- **Charity status line:** *Harbourside Community Trust is a registered charity in England and Wales, number [Registered charity number].* *Proposed — confirm the correct regulator, jurisdiction and wording.*
- **Company line, if we are a charitable company:** *Registered in England and Wales, company number [Company number]. Registered office: [Address].*
- **Copyright line:** *© [Year] Harbourside Community Trust. All rights reserved.* Use the year of first publication.

Put these lines on the website footer, letterhead, invoices, fundraising materials, emails and annual reports. Set them in the `small` type style, never smaller.

## Approvals workflow

1. **Everyday items** using an unchanged template (social posts, posters from templates, email signatures): the person making it checks it against this guide and the accessibility checklist. No sign-off needed.
2. **New designs** (new leaflets, campaigns, signage, merchandise): send a draft to the Communications team at [comms@harbourside.example](mailto:comms@harbourside.example). Allow five working days.
3. **Co-branded items and anything with a partner's or funder's logo**: Communications team approval, plus the partner's own approval process.
4. **Fundraising appeals, press releases and statements on sensitive issues**: Communications team plus a senior manager.
5. **Printed items over [budget threshold] or large runs**: see a printer's proof before sign-off.

> **Do** allow time for approval when planning a campaign or event.

> **Don't** print or publish a new design that has not been approved, even if it looks close to the templates.

## Asset library and file naming

The official files live in the asset kit that comes with this guide (listed below). Store a copy in our shared drive at **[shared drive location]** and always download from there, never from old emails or a website.

File names follow this pattern, in lower case with hyphens:

`[item]-[variant]-[colour]-[size].[format]`

For example, `primary-full-colour.png`, `primary-mono-light-knockout.svg` or `instagram-avatar-320x320.png`.

- Use **SVG** for websites and digital documents, **PNG** where SVG is not supported, and **vector PDF or EPS** for print once vector artwork is available.
- Our logo currently exists only as a PNG. Ask a designer to produce vector master artwork before large print, signage or merchandise.
