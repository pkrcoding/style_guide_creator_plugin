# Voice and tone

The people who read our words may be worried about rent, food or work. Some are older, and many speak English as a second language. Our voice must make them feel welcome, respected and sure of what to do next.

Our **voice** is who we are, and it stays the same everywhere. Our **tone** changes with the situation, the way a kind neighbour speaks differently at a party and at a hospital bedside.

*Proposed — confirm with leadership:* the voice attributes, tone settings and word list below. They are built from our mission, our four values and the personality in chapter 2.

## Voice attributes

We have four voice attributes. Each one links to a personality trait in chapter 2.

### 1. Warm, not sentimental

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Friendly, kind and welcoming. We talk to people as equals. | Gushing, pitying or dramatic. We never make people feel like a "case". |

**Example:** "Come along on Tuesday. We will make you a cup of tea, and someone will help you with the form."

### 2. Clear and simple, not childish

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Plain, direct and easy to translate. We use short sentences and everyday words. | Babyish, vague or full of jargon. Simple words do not mean simple ideas. |

**Example:** "You can get a food parcel if you live in Harbourside. Bring something with your address on it, such as a letter."

### 3. Calm and steady, not cold

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Reassuring and reliable. We say what will happen next. | Formal, distant or alarmed. We do not use official-sounding phrases or shout in capitals. |

**Example:** "If you are worried about losing your home, call us. We will listen, and we will help you work out your next step."

### 4. Hopeful and honest, not over-promising

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Positive about what people can do together. Open about what we can and cannot do. | Promising outcomes we cannot guarantee. Exaggerating need or results. |

**Example:** "We cannot find everyone a home straight away. But we will stay with you while you look."

> **Do** read your words aloud. If they sound like a friendly person from Harbourside, the voice is right.

> **Don't** borrow the voice of a bank, a council letter or a fundraising advert. We are none of those.

## Tone by context

Use this table to set your tone. The dials show where to sit for each situation:

- **Formality:** formal ↔ casual
- **Mood:** serious ↔ light

| Situation | Formality | Mood | Example | Words to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fundraising and campaigns** | Mid-casual | Mid-light, hopeful | "£10 helps us cook warm meals for families in Harbourside. Give if you can." | "Desperate", "shocking", "only you can", "before it's too late" |
| **Website** | Mid | Mid | "Need help with food? Here is how to get a food parcel this week." | "Service users", "beneficiaries", "eligibility criteria apply" |
| **Forms, buttons and messages (microcopy)** | Mid-casual | Neutral | Button: "Book a place". Hint: "We only use your phone number to contact you about this." | "Submit", "Invalid", "Required field" with no explanation |
| **Support for families** | Mid-casual | Serious and warm | "Thank you for telling us. We can help. Here is what happens next." | "Unfortunately", "As per", "You must" without a reason |
| **Error messages** | Mid | Neutral, calm | "We could not send your form. Check your internet connection and try again, or call us." | "Error 500", "Something went wrong" with no next step, "You entered" (blame) |
| **Bad news and crisis** | Mid-formal | Serious | "Our Tuesday food club is closed this week because of flooding. Your nearest open point is [place, time]." | Jokes, emoji, "We regret to inform you", guesses about cause or blame |
| **Social posts** | Casual | Light to mid | "Thank you to everyone who helped at Saturday's community lunch. 60 hot meals served." | Idioms, slang, more than 3 hashtags, emoji in the middle of sentences |
| **Social replies and complaints** | Mid-casual | Serious and kind | "We're sorry this happened. Please message us privately so we can put it right." | Arguing in public, copy-and-paste replies, asking for personal details in public |
| **Volunteer recruitment** | Casual | Light, welcoming | "Do you have two hours a week? Help us pack food boxes on Thursday mornings. No experience needed." | "Rockstar", "ninja", "must be young and energetic" |
| **Council partners, legal and funders** | Formal | Serious | "We propose a joint drop-in for housing advice. We have set out the costs below." | Unsupported claims, jargon without definitions, "world-class" |
| **Internal communications** | Casual | Mid | "Quick update: the warm-space rota for November is now on the shared drive." | Acronyms nobody has defined, blame |

The figure "60 hot meals" in the social example shows the format only. Always use real, checked numbers.

> **Note** We are a charity, not a company, so we have no investors. Write to funders, grant-makers and the council with the "Council partners, legal and funders" tone.

### Crisis and bad news checklist

When something goes wrong, people need facts fast. Follow these steps:

1. Say what has happened in the first sentence.
2. Say what it means for the reader.
3. Say what they should do now, and where to get help.
4. Say when we will update them.
5. Get approval from the Communications team before you post.

> **Don't** use humour, emoji or campaign hashtags during a crisis or when someone has made a complaint.

## Writing about people and money

Our audiences include people who are struggling and people who are giving. Both deserve respect.

- **Dignity first.** Describe a situation, not a person: "families who need help with food", not "the needy".
- **No guilt.** Ask donors to give because they can make a difference, not because they should feel bad.
- **Real stories need real consent.** Only share a person's story, name or photo with their written consent. Tell them where it will appear and for how long. They can change their mind at any time. Chapter 12 has the full rules.

> **Do** show people as active: "Amira found a job through our work club." (Use real names only with consent.)

> **Don't** show people as helpless: "Poor Amira had nowhere to turn until we rescued her."

## Before and after

These rewrites show the voice in our own work. The "after" versions use shorter sentences, everyday words and a clear next step.

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Service users meeting the eligibility criteria may access emergency food provision via referral." | "If you need food this week, we can help. Call us or visit any Tuesday from 10am to 12pm." | Plain words, a direct "you", and a clear action |
| "Without YOUR help, these desperate families will go hungry this winter." | "This winter, £15 helps a family in Harbourside share a warm meal. Will you help?" | Hopeful, not guilt-based; no capitals for emphasis |
| "We are thrilled to announce our exciting new partnership synergy with the council!" | "We are working with the council to give housing advice at our Tuesday drop-in." | Says what is happening, without hype |
| "Volunteers are needed ASAP to help the less fortunate. Get stuck in!" | "We need volunteers to pack food boxes on Thursday mornings. Can you give two hours?" | No idioms, no pitying labels, a specific request |
| "Your application could not be processed due to missing information." | "We need one more thing: a letter with your address on it. You can bring it to us or send a photo on WhatsApp." | Calm, active and tells the reader exactly what to do |

The times, amounts and services in these examples show the style only. Check every real detail with the Communications team before you publish.

## Words we use and avoid

| We say | We avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| families, people, neighbours, residents | the needy, the poor, the less fortunate, service users, clients | Labels take away dignity |
| people on a low income | poor people, deprived people | Describes a situation, not a person |
| people who are homeless, people without a safe home | the homeless, vagrants, rough sleepers (as a label) | People first |
| food parcel, food support | handouts, charity hand-out, freebies | "Handout" suggests a favour, not support |
| people claiming benefits, people getting Universal Credit | benefit scroungers, welfare dependants, on the dole | Stigmatising and untrue |
| help, support, work with | rescue, save, give a voice to | People are not helpless |
| give, donate | "only you can save", "before it's too late" | No guilt or false urgency |
| volunteer | helper (as a lesser term) | Volunteers are equal members of the team |
| the council, our council partners | the authorities | Warmer and specific |

> **Accessibility** Use the same word for the same thing every time. If you call it a "food parcel" on the poster, do not call it a "food box" on the website. Readers using translation tools and readers with English as a second language rely on this.
